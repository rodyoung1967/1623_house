House History Evidence Report
1623 NE 73rd AvePortland, Oregon 97213
Evidence assembled through August 16, 2026

Purpose. This report separates documented facts, strong inferences, oral-history evidence, and unresolved hypotheses. It is intentionally conservative: where the evidence does not prove a point, the report says so.
Most important correction from the current evidence set: the earliest plumbing card is dated September 1912, not 1913. It describes a “1 story frame new” dwelling and names the owner as “Mr. J. Deveny.” This is the strongest evidence yet that the DeVeny connection began at or essentially at original construction.

Prepared as a working historical record; not a title report or legal ownership opinion.

Executive Summary
5/5: The property is Lots 27 and 28, Block 33, Jonesmore. This legal description appears on the historic city records.
5/5: A plumbing inspection began September 2, 1912 and was finalized September 13, 1912. The card describes a “1 story frame new” dwelling and names the owner as “Mr. J. Deveny.”
4/5: The 1912 “Mr. J. Deveny” is likely a member of the same DeVeny family later associated with the house; identifying him specifically as James Benjamin DeVeny remains probable rather than proven.
5/5: In 1939 the house was converted from a cesspool to the city sewer under sewer permit 41973.
5/5: A 1947 plumbing record names “Mrs. Deveny” as owner and documents replacement of one water service.
5/5: A 1966 City of Portland oil-burner permit at 1623 NE 73rd was installed for “E. A. DeVeny” and shows a 275-gallon basement fuel tank.
5/5: Edgar A. DeVeny died April 24, 1969; an Oregon obituary index gives 1623 NE 73rd Ave as his death place.
3/5: Fern A. DeVeny may be the older woman a neighbor remembers living alone in the house after Edgar’s death and possibly until Fern died in 1986. This fits the evidence, but we do not yet have a 1980s directory, obituary, death certificate, or deed proving Fern’s residence at 1623 immediately before her death.
4/5 oral history: The house was tenant-occupied when Christine M. Irvin bought it in 1992, based on the current owner’s firsthand knowledge. The start date of the rental period is not yet documented.
5/5 sale event; 4/5 buyer identity: A public-record aggregation reports a sale on December 1, 1992 for $64,950. The current owner identifies Christine M. Irvin as the buyer; the deed itself has not yet been added to this evidence set.
5/5 sale event: A public-record aggregation reports a sale on May 27, 1999 for $127,000. The current owner recalls buying from Christine in summer 1999; the exact closing/recording date should ultimately be reconciled against the deed.
Chronological Evidence Ledger
1907
Jonesmore was actively marketed for residential development.
Evidence: A September 1907 Oregonian advertisement by George D. Schalk offered buyers a Jonesmore lot and said his organization would build them a home. This is neighborhood context, not proof Schalk built this house.
Certainty: 5/5 for context; 1/5 as a builder attribution
Sources: W6
Qualification: No property-specific builder record has been found.

1912
The house was a new one-story frame dwelling and a DeVeny was named as owner.
Evidence: Historic City of Portland plumbing inspection: first inspection 9/2/1912; final inspection 9/13/1912; “1 story frame new”; “Dwelling”; owner line reads “Mr. J. Deveny.” The card also identifies Lots 27 & 28, Block 33, Jonesmore.
Certainty: 5/5 for date, building status, legal description, and DeVeny owner name; 4/5 that the first initial is J
Sources: P1
Qualification: The handwritten first initial is somewhat stylized but reads most plausibly as J. Identifying this person specifically as James Benjamin DeVeny remains unproven.

1912
Public property data also gives 1912 as the year built.
Evidence: Realtor.com’s public-record property history lists “Built 1912,” along with 1,102 square feet and a 5,724-square-foot lot.
Certainty: 4/5
Sources: W1
Qualification: Useful corroboration, but the 1912 city plumbing card is the stronger primary source for original construction timing.

1912 context
North Jonesmore was in an active building boom.
Evidence: The April 7, 1912 Sunday Oregonian reported that a large number of attractive homes had been built in North Jonesmore and that street grading and cement sidewalk work were underway.
Certainty: 5/5 for neighborhood context
Sources: W7
Qualification: This does not identify the builder or owner of 1623 specifically.

Pre-1933 address
The 1912 plumbing card appears to preserve an earlier street number, “369,” beneath/alongside the modern address.
Evidence: The scan carries both the modern “1623 N.E.” notation and a handwritten “369.” Portland comprehensively renumbered addresses in the early 1930s, finishing in 1933.
Certainty: 3/5
Sources: P1, W10
Qualification: The exact pre-1933 address format should be verified in the official street/name change directory before it is stated as definitive.

1939
The property was connected to the city sewer and changed from a cesspool system.
Evidence: Historic plumbing permit dated 9/26/1939 states “Change from cesspool to sewer,” lists sewer permit 41973, and describes the building as a “1 story old frame res.”
Certainty: 5/5
Sources: P2

1939
The sewer record independently matches the legal description and permit number.
Evidence: City of Portland Department of Public Works record for permit 41973 identifies 1623 NE 73rd Ave, Jonesmore, Lots 27–28, Block 33, between Halsey Street and Broadway, with Geo. L. Melvine as applicant.
Certainty: 5/5
Sources: P4
Qualification: The form’s handwritten dates are difficult in places; the linked 1939 plumbing record provides the clearest event date.

1939
A city sketch documents the sewer/service geometry around the property.
Evidence: A companion historic drawing shows NE 73rd with Broadway and Halsey and a service line/location sketch.
Certainty: 4/5
Sources: P5
Qualification: The sketch is clearly associated with the address set, but it does not by itself identify an owner.

1947
A woman recorded as “Mrs. Deveny” was the owner when a water service was replaced.
Evidence: City plumbing inspection dated 2/7/1947, permit 185319, names “Mrs. Deveny” as owner, calls the building an “Old dwelling,” and records “Water Service 1 repl.” Final inspection appears 2/14/1947.
Certainty: 5/5
Sources: P3
Qualification: Which Mrs. DeVeny this was is not yet proven. It should not be assigned to Sophia or Fern without a directory/deed link.

1966
“E. A. DeVeny” was directly associated with the house and oil heat.
Evidence: City of Portland Fire Prevention Division oil-burner permit at 1623 NE 73rd, dated in July 1966, states “Install for E. A. DeVeny,” marks warm-air heat, and shows a 275-gallon tank located in the basement.
Certainty: 5/5 for the permit facts; 4/5 that E.A. is Edgar A. DeVeny
Sources: P6
Qualification: The reviewed permit documents a basement tank, not an underground tank. The user recalls additional underground-storage-tank material, which should be added when available.

1969
Edgar A. DeVeny died while directly tied to 1623 NE 73rd Ave.
Evidence: VA-derived cemetery data gives Edgar A. DeVeny’s death as 4/24/1969; the Oregon obituary index gives his death place as 1623 NE 73rd Ave.
Certainty: 5/5
Sources: W2, W3

1986
Fern A. Farmer DeVeny died July 18, 1986.
Evidence: Find a Grave lists Fern A. DeVeny, spouse of Edgar Arthur DeVeny, born 1/29/1906 and died 7/18/1986.
Certainty: 4/5
Sources: W4
Qualification: This establishes Fern’s death date and spouse relationship in a secondary genealogical source, but does not establish her residence at 1623 in 1986.

1969–1986?
Fern may have remained in the house alone after Edgar’s death.
Evidence: A neighbor remembers an “old lady” who lived in the house alone for as long as the neighbor knew her. Fern was Edgar’s widow and lived until 1986, which makes the identification plausible.
Certainty: 3/5
Sources: O2, W4
Qualification: The neighbor memory is strong evidence that an older woman lived alone there; identifying that woman as Fern and extending her residence all the way to 1986 remain inferences.

Nola — later life
Nola DeVeny later used the surnames Fick and Dowty; Nola Diane Dowty died October 9, 2000.
Evidence: A genealogical index associates the names Nola Diane DeVeny, Nola D. Fick, and Nola Diane Dowty. VA-derived Willamette National Cemetery data lists Nola Diane Dowty, wife of Lloyd W. Dowty, died 10/9/2000.
Certainty: 4/5 for name continuity/death date
Sources: W5, W11
Qualification: Her birth year conflicts between sources (1930 in one index, 1933 in the VA-derived cemetery database). The report does not resolve that discrepancy.

Before 1992
Nola had moved away from the house before her death; exact move-out date is unknown.
Evidence: This is the current owner’s recollection from prior family/house history research, and Nola’s later married-name records are consistent with her living elsewhere.
Certainty: 2/5
Sources: O1, W5, W11
Qualification: No city directory, voter record, deed, or obituary has yet established the year she stopped residing at 1623.

1986–1992?
The house may have become a rental after Fern’s death and remained a rental until the 1992 sale.
Evidence: This hypothesis fits Fern’s 1986 death, the neighbor’s memory of an older woman living alone, and the current owner’s certainty that the property was tenant-occupied when Christine bought it.
Certainty: 3/5
Sources: O1, O2, W4
Qualification: Only the rental status at the time of Christine’s purchase is treated as strong firsthand evidence. The rental start date is not yet proven; “1986–1992” is a working hypothesis.

Highest-Priority Unresolved Questions
Who exactly was the “Mr. J. Deveny” on the September 1912 plumbing card? A 1912 city directory, 1910/1920 census address match, or the original deed would be decisive.
Who held legal title between 1912 and 1992? A complete Multnomah County deed-index chain can establish whether ownership was truly continuous in the DeVeny family.
Who was “Mrs. Deveny” on the 1947 permit? It should not be assigned to a particular family member without a contemporaneous directory or deed.
Did Fern live at 1623 continuously after Edgar’s 1969 death and until her own death in 1986? A 1969–1986 reverse directory run or Fern’s death certificate/obituary could answer this.
When did the house become a rental, and who rented it? Reverse address directories and classified ads from 1986–1992 are the best path.
Who conveyed the house to Christine M. Irvin in 1992? The deed is the single highest-value missing document for the late ownership chain.
What is the exact original/pre-1933 address? The 1912 plumbing card appears to show “369,” but the official 1933 address-change directory should be checked.
Was there an underground storage tank in addition to the documented 275-gallon basement tank? The additional tank records recalled by the owner should be located and added.
Who built the house? George D. Schalk is a neighborhood development lead, but there is no property-specific builder attribution yet.
Record-access path. Multnomah County’s public research area provides recorded documents from 1955 to current and a deed index from 1855 to current. This should make the 1992 deed and earlier title chain research feasible. Portland also directs researchers to Historic Plumbing records through PortlandMaps. [W8, W9]
