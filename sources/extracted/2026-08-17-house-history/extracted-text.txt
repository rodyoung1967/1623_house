House History — Evidence-Based Working Edition
Updated 17 August 2026
Purpose
This edition combines the municipal permit evidence, the earlier house-history notes, current-owner and neighbor oral history, and the newly recovered Oregon newspaper clippings. Every historical assertion is paired with a source reference, and uncertain points are labeled rather than silently filled in.
Bottom line. The DeVeny connection to the property is documented at original construction in September 1912 and again repeatedly through 1969. Edgar A. DeVeny was unquestionably residing at 1623 NE 73rd at his death in 1969. Oral history strongly suggests Fern remained there alone until her death in 1986, after which the house likely became a rental before the December 1992 sale. The exact legal title chain, Fern’s final residence, and the identity of the 1986–1992 tenants remain the most important unresolved questions.
Sources: P1, P2–P6, N6–N7, O1–O2, W1, W4. Qualifications are detailed below.

The House and the DeVeny Family
1912: construction and the first DeVeny connection
The strongest surviving evidence for the house’s origin is a City of Portland plumbing inspection begun September 2, 1912 and finalized September 13. The card describes the building as a “1 story frame new” dwelling, gives the legal location as Lots 27 and 28, Block 33, Jonesmore, and names the owner as “Mr. J. Deveny.” This establishes a DeVeny owner at essentially the moment of construction.
Source: P1 — City of Portland historic plumbing inspection, September 1912.
The earlier house history identified this man as James Benjamin DeVeny (1871–1945) and his wife Sophia Charlotte Goodwin DeVeny. That identification remains probable rather than proved: the municipal card gives only “Mr. J. Deveny,” and no original deed or 1912 directory entry has yet been added that explicitly connects James Benjamin to this parcel.
Sources: P1; H1 — earlier house-history research; W12 — James Benjamin DeVeny genealogical memorial. Certainty: 3/5 for the specific James Benjamin identification.
The property sat in Jonesmore/North Jonesmore during a period of active residential development. Contemporary Oregonian material shows Jonesmore lots being marketed with home-building arrangements in 1907 and reports substantial home construction and street improvements in North Jonesmore in 1912.
Sources: W6 — Sunday Oregonian, 29 Sept. 1907; W7 — Sunday Oregonian, 7 Apr. 1912. Context only.
1939–1947: sewer and water modernization
By 1939 the house was an established “old frame” residence. A city plumbing permit records the change from a cesspool to the sanitary sewer, and the matching Public Works record identifies sewer permit 41973, the Jonesmore legal description, and the block between Halsey and Broadway. A companion field sketch shows the service geometry.
Sources: P2 — 1939 plumbing inspection; P4 — Public Works sewer permit 41973; P5 — sewer/service sketch. Certainty: 5/5.
In February 1947, another plumbing inspection calls the property an “Old dwelling,” names “Mrs. Deveny” as owner, and records replacement of one water service. The permit does not specify whether this Mrs. DeVeny was Fern or another family member, so the identity should remain open.
Source: P3 — City plumbing inspection, permit 185319, 7 Feb. 1947, final inspection apparently 14 Feb. 1947. Certainty: 5/5 for permit facts; identity unresolved.
James Benjamin DeVeny had left the house by 1945
A newly recovered 1945 funeral notice for James Benjamin DeVeny reports that he died at his home at 0214 SW Dakota Street. Whatever his exact role in the 1912 property, this establishes that James was living elsewhere by 1945.
Source: N8 — “James B. DeVeny” funeral notice, 1 Nov. 1945, owner-supplied newspaper clipping; publication title still to be verified. Certainty: 5/5 for the address stated in the clipping.
Edgar, Fern, Irene, and Nola
The earlier research notes state that the 1940 U.S. Census lists Edgar Arthur DeVeny, wife Fern, and daughters Irene and Nola at the house. Because the original census page is not currently in the audited evidence set, this edition treats the household identification as probable rather than fully confirmed. Importantly, a census can support occupancy and owner/renter status, but it is not by itself a deed chain.
Sources: C1 — 1940 U.S. Census as cited in H1; H1 — earlier house-history notes. Certainty: 3/5 until the original census image is re-added and checked.
Nola’s marriages and household away from 1623
The newly recovered newspaper clippings correct an older family-history error. Nola’s 1950 marriage was not to Lloyd Dowty. On December 31, 1950, Nola Diane DeVeny married Jack Howard Fick. The January 12, 1951 Oregon Journal caption names her parents as Mr. and Mrs. E. A. DeVeny.
Source: N1 — Oregon Journal, 12 Jan. 1951, p. 15, “Recent Bride—Mrs. Jack Howard Fick (Nola Diane DeVeny).” Certainty: 5/5.
By September 1952 that first marriage had ended, and Nola married Lloyd Wallace Dowty. The Oregon Journal’s September 26 article identifies her as Nola Diane DeVeny, daughter of Mr. and Mrs. E. A. DeVeny, and Lloyd as the son of Mr. and Mrs. Willard D. Dowty. Because the article says the wedding occurred on the preceding Saturday, the marriage date is September 20, 1952. The manner and date by which the Fick marriage ended remain undocumented.
Source: N2 — Oregon Journal, 26 Sept. 1952, “Nola D. DeVeny Weds Saturday.” Certainty: 5/5 for the Dowty marriage; unresolved for the Fick marriage termination.
The Dowtys’ first child, Bruce Edgar, was born October 2, 1954. Their daughter Diane Fern was born February 8, 1957. A 1967 notice records the birth of Denise Genevieve and explicitly describes Mr. and Mrs. Lloyd Wallace Dowty as “of Milwaukie.” This proves Nola had an established household away from 1623 by 1967.
Sources: N3 — Oregon Journal, 6 Oct. 1954; N4 — Oregon Journal, 15 Feb. 1957; N5 — Oregon Journal, 7 Jun. 1967, p. 18. Certainty: 5/5 for the events and 1967 Milwaukie residence.
1966–1969: Edgar at 1623 and the oil-heating era
A July 1966 City of Portland Fire Prevention Division oil-burner permit at 1623 NE 73rd states “Install for E. A. DeVeny,” identifies warm-air heat, and shows a 275-gallon tank in the basement. The permit documents a basement tank; it does not establish the separate underground-storage-tank question that remains in the research files.
Source: P6 — City of Portland Fire Prevention Division oil-burner permit, July 1966. Certainty: 5/5 for permit facts; 4/5 that E. A. is Edgar A. DeVeny.
Edgar’s 1969 funeral notices provide the clearest late-life address evidence. One notice reads “DEVENY — Edgar A., 1623 NE 73rd Ave.” Another identifies him at the same address as husband of Fern A. and father of Irene F. Taylor and Nola D. Dowty. A VA-derived cemetery transcription gives Edgar’s death date as April 24, 1969. These sources establish that 1623 was Edgar’s listed residence at death; they do not, by themselves, prove that he physically died inside the house.
Sources: N6 — Oregon Journal funeral notice, 26 Apr. 1969; N7 — The Oregonian funeral notice, 28 Apr. 1969; W3 — Willamette National Cemetery/VA-derived transcription. Certainty: 5/5 for residence at death and family identifications.
1969–1986: Fern alone — strong oral history, incomplete documentation
The longstanding neighbor account says that after Edgar died, Fern remained in the house alone and that she died in the house in 1986. The earlier notes further record that the neighbor knew Fern and specifically identified the “old lady” remembered by the later owners as Fern rather than a different tenant. This is stronger than a generic neighborhood recollection, but it remains oral history until a late city directory, obituary address, death record, or deed independently places Fern at 1623 immediately before her death.
Sources: O2 — next-door neighbor recollection as preserved in H1; H1 — earlier house-history notes. Certainty: 3/5 for continuous residence through 1986 and death in the house.
A secondary genealogical source gives Fern A. Farmer DeVeny’s death as July 18, 1986 and identifies Edgar as her spouse. This establishes the death date reasonably well, but not the place of death.
Source: W4 — Find a Grave, Fern A. Farmer DeVeny. Certainty: 4/5 for date/spouse; residence not established by this source.
1986–1992: likely rental period
The best current reconstruction is that 1623 became a rental after Fern’s death. The neighbor account says Nola did not move back into the house with Lloyd and her family, while the current owner is certain that the property was tenant-occupied when the Irvin family acquired it in 1992. What is not yet known is whether the rental began immediately after Fern’s death, who the tenants were, or whether there was any short period of vacancy.
Sources: O1 — current-owner firsthand account; O2 — neighbor recollection; H1 — earlier house-history notes. Certainty: 3/5 for 1986–1992 as the full rental period; 4/5 that it was tenant-occupied at the 1992 acquisition.
1992: end of the DeVeny/Dowty era
Public-record history reports a sale on December 1, 1992 for $64,950. The earlier house-history research identifies the parties as Nola D. Dowty, grantor, and Wayne Irvin et al., grantee. The current owner has clarified that Wayne Irvin was Christine M. Irvin’s father. That family relationship makes it plausible that Christine was included in the “et al.” grantee group, but only the recorded deed can establish the exact ownership form and every grantee.
Sources: W1 — Realtor.com public-record history, sale date and price; H1 — earlier house-history notes, party names; O1 — current-owner statement that Wayne Irvin was Christine’s father. Certainty: 5/5 for sale date/price; 3/5 for exact parties until deed is retrieved.
1992–1999 and the current ownership era
According to the current owner’s firsthand knowledge, Christine did not continue using the property as a rental after the 1992 acquisition. Public-record history reports a later sale on May 27, 1999 for $127,000. The current owner recalls buying the property from Christine in summer 1999. The recorded deed or closing statement should be used to reconcile the public-record date with the remembered closing/possession timing.
Sources: O1 — current-owner firsthand account; W1 — Realtor.com public-record history; H1 — earlier notes identifying Christine as seller. Certainty: 5/5 for the recorded sale event/price; 4/5 for seller identity and exact timing pending deed.
