# House history: 1623 NE 73rd Ave

A sourced narrative of **1623 NE 73rd Ave, Portland, OR 97213**. This is not a title report. Every claim has a source ID. IDs are defined in [sources.md](sources.md). Working readings and open conflicts are in [decisions.md](decisions.md) and [CONFLICTS.md](CONFLICTS.md).

Oral history is labeled **oral**. Census and city directories are evidence of occupancy/tenure, not substitutes for recorded deeds ([decisions.md](decisions.md) C05).

The house stands on **Lots 27 and 28, Block 33, Jonesmore**, between Halsey and Broadway (P1, P4). The current Multnomah tax account is **R193918** (S2026b; county printout still wanted). Public-record aggregators list it as built 1912, about **1,102 sq ft**, 2 bed, 1 bath, on a **5,724 sq ft** lot (W1, W15).

The builder is unknown. In 1907 George D. Schalk advertised Jonesmore lots and offered to build for buyers (W6). That is neighborhood marketing, not a builder attribution for this house. In April 1912 the *Sunday Oregonian* reported a building boom in North Jonesmore (W7). That article does not name this lot.

## 1912: a new one-story frame dwelling

A City of Portland plumbing card records a **new one-story frame dwelling** on this legal description (P1). First inspection **2 Mar 1912**, final **13 Mar 1913** ([decisions.md](decisions.md) C01). The card lists dwelling use and handwritten fixture and drain notes (P1).

The owner line on the same card is **Mr. J. Deveny** ([decisions.md](decisions.md) C02). Identifying that man as **James Benjamin DeVeny (1871–1945)** remains a strong family lead, not proof (C03; S2022, W12, W35).

Newspaper evidence improves the family reconstruction without closing C03. On **21 Sep 1920**, the *Oregon Journal* reported the death of **James Goodwin** and identified his daughter as **Mrs. Sophia DeVeny of 102 Dakota Street** (W34). This resolves Sophia's family surname as **Goodwin**, not the later family-tree spelling “Godwin” ([decisions.md](decisions.md) C16). James Benjamin DeVeny's 1945 *Oregonian* obituary independently identifies sons including **E. A., L. R., and Howard DeVeny** (W35). Neither clipping links James Benjamin to this parcel in 1912; a deed or contemporary directory is still required (C03).

The plumbing card also shows an earlier street number **369**, later overwritten or annotated as **1623 N.E.** (P1). Portland finished citywide renumbering in July 1933 (W10). The 2022 notes give the full old form as **369 E 73rd Ave N** (S2022). The number 369 is certain; the exact old street string is still open (C04).

## 1939–1947: sewer, census, and water

Before 1939 the house used a cesspool (P2). On **26 Sep 1939** it was changed to city sewer, sewer permit **41973** (P2, P4, P5). The building was described as a one-story old frame residence (P2). The Public Works record matches this address and Jonesmore Lots 27–28, Block 33; applicant **Geo. L. Melvine** (P4). The owner line on the 1939 plumbing card is spelled **“Mr. De Verney”** (P2).

### 1940: Edgar and Fern's household — original census page now in hand

The original 1940 census schedule is now archived as P7. It is **ED 37-308, sheet 1A**, enumerated **2 Apr 1940**; the DeVeny household appears on **lines 16–19**. House number **1623** is visible (P7).

| Person | Relation | Age | Birthplace | Source |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Edgar DeVeny | Head | 44 | Oregon | P7 |
| Fern DeVeny | Wife | 34 | Idaho | P7 |
| Irene DeVeny | Daughter | 17 | Oregon | P7 |
| Nola DeVeny | Daughter | 6 | Oregon | P7 |

The census row reports **O (owned)**, home value **$1,500**, and **No** for farm (P7). That is strong contemporary evidence that Edgar's household reported owning the home. It is still not treated as a recorded deed or chain-of-title instrument (C05).

A **1 Nov 1945** funeral notice for James B. DeVeny gives his home as **0214 SW Dakota Street**, not 1623 (W33). The fuller *Oregonian* obituary of **2 Nov 1945** confirms the Dakota Street home, says he was born **14 Aug 1871 in Memphis, Tennessee**, came to Portland about 61 years earlier, and names sons including E. A. DeVeny (W35). This supports the family relationship but does not settle the identity of the 1912 `Mr. J. Deveny` (C03).

On **7 Feb 1947** a water-service replacement was inspected (permit **185319**); final **14 Feb 1947** (P3). The owner on that card is **“Mrs. Deveny.”** The work is certain. Fern is a strong candidate because the 1940 and later records put her in the household, but the card itself does not give a first name (C06).

## 1950–1952: Nola leaves the parental household

An Ancestry index of the original 1950 schedule (ED **37-206**, census day **1 Apr 1950**) lists **1623 N. E 73** (P8):

| Person (indexed) | Relation | Age | Notes | Source |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Edgar A. Deveny | Head | 54 | Printer, private printing company | P8 |
| Fenn A. Deveny | Wife | 44 | Indexed **Fenn** = Fern | P8 |
| Nala D. Deveny | Daughter | 16 | Indexed **Nala** = Nola; unmarried | P8 |

Irene is **not** in the household. Nola was still living at 1623 and unmarried eight months before her first marriage (P8).

On **31 Dec 1950**, **Nola Diane DeVeny** married **Jack Howard Fick**, son of the George S. Ficks (*Oregon Journal*, 12 Jan 1951, p. 15) (W26). How that marriage ended remains undocumented.

A major new address anchor comes from the *Oregonian* marriage-license column of **4 Nov 1952**: it lists **Lloyd W. Dowty, 4807 N.E. 75th**, and **Nola D. DeVeny, 1623 N.E. 73d** (W36). Thus Nola was again using 1623 as her residence immediately before her second marriage.

On **8 Nov 1952**, Nola married **Lloyd Wallace Dowty** at Grant Park Baptist Church. The *Oregonian* wedding article of **11 Nov 1952** explicitly gives the date, identifies her as **Nola Diane DeVeny**, daughter of **Mr. and Mrs. Edgar A. DeVeny**, and says the newlyweds would make their home in Portland after a California trip (W37). This supersedes the archive's earlier September-1952 inference from W30/S2026c ([decisions.md](decisions.md) C17).

## 1954–1969: Edgar and Fern remain; Nola's household is elsewhere

Nola and Lloyd's children are documented in contemporary newspaper notices:

| Child | Birth evidence | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bruce Edgar (1957 notice: Bruce Lloyd) | **2 Oct 1954**, Wilcox Memorial | W31, W32 |
| Diane Fern | **8 Feb 1957**, Wilcox Memorial | W32 |
| Denise Genevieve | newspapers disagree: **23 May 1967** (W29) vs **25 May 1967** (W38) | W29, W38; C18 |

The two Denise notices agree on the child, siblings Bruce and Diane, and the DeVeny/Dowty grandparents; they disagree by two days on the birth date. W29 additionally identifies Lloyd and Nola's household as **of Milwaukie**. The birth date remains open pending a primary record (C18).

Polk city directories list **Edgar A. (Fern A.)** at **1623 NE 73d** in 1956, 1957, and 1959 (W23, W24, W25). In 1957 Edgar is a **printer at Ivy Press** (W24). The 1959 reverse list puts **DeVeny Edgar A.** at 1623, phone **AL 2-4971** (W25). These are occupancy records, not deeds.

Portland Maps aerials show the house on the west side of NE 73rd from **1962** through **2025**, with **1611** immediately south (M1962–M2025). A smaller rear structure is visible at least by 1996 (M1996).

In **1966** a Fire Prevention Division oil-burner permit at this address records **warm-air oil heat** and a **275-gallon basement tank**, installed for **E. A. DeVeny** (P6). The permit facts are certain; that E.A. is Edgar is 4/5. Whether Fern had a legal title interest at that point and whether the permit month is July or September remain open (C07, C08).

### 1969: Edgar dies at 1623

**Edgar A. DeVeny** died **24 Apr 1969** (W3, W13). The *Oregon Journal* death notice of **26 Apr 1969** gives his residence as **1623 NE 73rd Ave**, names Fern A. as his wife and Irene F. Taylor and Nola D. Dowty as daughters, and announces vault interment at Willamette National Cemetery (W27). A shorter funeral notice on **28 Apr 1969** repeats the address (W28).

This is a strong address-specific anchor: Edgar was still associated with 1623 at death. The notices identify Irene and Nola as daughters, not as occupants of the house.

## 1969–1986: Fern is the likely elderly woman remembered at the house

After Edgar's death, Fern survived another 17 years. The strongest current interpretation of the oral and documentary evidence is that **the elderly woman remembered by the neighbor as living alone at 1623 was very likely Fern A. DeVeny, not Nola** ([decisions.md](decisions.md) C19).

Why that identification is strong:

- the neighbor said they **knew Fern** and identified the older woman as Fern (O2);
- Edgar's 1969 notices put him at 1623 immediately before Fern became a widow (W27, W28);
- Nola and Lloyd were documented **of Milwaukie in 1967** (W29);
- Irene Taylor's 1987 obituary again identifies **Nola Dowty of Milwaukie** (W40).

The same neighbor also remembered Fern as having **died in the house**. That detail is now contradicted by documentary evidence. Fern's *Oregonian* obituary of **22 Jul 1986** states that she **died 18 Jul 1986 at Friendship Care Center**, age 80 (W39). Therefore the unresolved question is **when Fern stopped living at 1623 and entered care**, not where she died (C10 resolved; see `needed-records.md`).

W39 adds family and biographical detail: Fern had been married to Edgar for 48 years, had lived in Portland since her youth, was active in square-dance clubs for many years, enjoyed gardening, and was survived by daughters **Irene Fern Taylor of Portland** and **Nola Diane Dowty of Milwaukie**. The obituary says Fern was born in **Cambridge, Idaho**, while W14/Find a Grave says **Midvale, Washington County, Idaho**; birthplace remains open as C20.

## 1986–1992: the gap

What is now documented or strongly supported:

- Edgar's 1969 notices give residence **1623** (W27, W28).
- The elderly long-time woman remembered at the house was very likely **Fern**, not Nola (O2, W29, W40; C19).
- Fern died **18 Jul 1986 at Friendship Care Center**, not at the house (W39).
- Nola was **of Milwaukie in 1987** (W40).
- Current owner recollection: the house was **tenant-occupied when Christine bought it in 1992** (O1).

Still unknown:

- Fern's **last date living at 1623** and when she entered care;
- the first post-Fern occupant or tenant;
- whether the rental period began immediately in 1986 or later;
- the recorded path of title from Fern's estate to Nola;
- tenant names in 1986–1992.

These are now the highest-value collection targets in [`needed-records.md`](needed-records.md).

## 1992–1999: Irvin purchase, then Young

Public records report a sale on **1 Dec 1992** for **$64,950** (W1). That date and price match the 2022 notes (S2022). Working record until the deed is in hand: **Nola D. Dowty** sold to **Wayne Irvin et al.** (S2022, W1). Wayne is **Christine M. Irvin's father** (O1). Christine was likely among the “et al.” grantees; the recorded instruments must confirm the exact grantee list and title form ([decisions.md](decisions.md) C12). Randy's legal role remains open (C13).

The current owner says the house was tenant-occupied at that purchase and that Christine did **not** rent it afterward (O1). Tenant names are unknown.

Sometime **1992–1999**, an unnamed informant said the oil tank was removed and **given to the owners of 1611 NE 73rd**, likely when a gas furnace was installed (O4). That tank was the **275-gallon basement tank** documented in 1966 (P6). There was **no underground storage tank** at 1623 ([decisions.md](decisions.md) C09).

Public records report a sale on **27 May 1999** for **$127,000** (W1). The 2022 notes give **1 May 1999**, **Rodney A. Young ET AL**, from **Christine M. Irvin**, same price (S2022). The owner recalls summer or July 1999 (O1). Price is accepted; the exact legal/recording date and identity of the additional grantee(s) remain open (C14).
